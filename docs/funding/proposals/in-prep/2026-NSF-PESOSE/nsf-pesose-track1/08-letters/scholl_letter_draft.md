**Re: Letter of Collaboration for the FreeMoCap PESOSE Proposal**

To Whom It May Concern:

I am writing on behalf of [Laboratory / Institution] regarding our ongoing collaboration with the FreeMoCap Foundation.

Our laboratory studies the development of natural visual and sensorimotor behavior in freely moving animals, with a current focus on juvenile ferrets during the period following eye opening. This work requires simultaneous measurement of multiple aspects of natural behavior and neural activity, including binocular eye movements, three-dimensional head and body movement, and neural recordings from visual cortex.

Our work with juvenile ferrets has required us to adapt markerless motion-capture methods beyond standard human pose-estimation workflows. As part of this work, we have collaborated with the FreeMoCap team to reconstruct and analyze three-dimensional movement from synchronized behavioral cameras. Our experimental arena uses multiple cameras to capture freely moving juvenile ferrets while additional systems measure eye movements and neural activity. Working with the FreeMoCap team has allowed us to build on existing open-source tools while developing a custom processing pipeline suited to our experimental setup. 

One component of this pipeline is SkellyClicker, an open-source tool that makes custom DeepLabCut tracking models easier to create and use within motion-capture workflows. The FreeMoCap team led its development, while members of our laboratory contributed to its design, testing, and use through our ferret-tracking work. Because SkellyClicker supports custom tracking models, the same workflow can be adapted for other researchers working with nonhuman animals or other tracking problems that are not well served by standard human pose-estimation models.

If the proposed PESOSE project is funded, we anticipate continuing to participate in the FreeMoCap ecosystem as both scientific users and contributors. Our laboratory expects to continue contributing to the development and evaluation of tools and workflows for nonhuman tracking, while providing feedback on technical interfaces, documentation, and contributor processes that can make specialized extensions easier to develop and share.

We expect this collaboration to continue informing tools that are useful not only for our own research, but also for other researchers with specialized motion-tracking needs.
