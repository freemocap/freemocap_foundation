# Research-Software Sustainability (the abandonware mechanism)

The **documented, citable** reason academic tools decay — and the backbone of the "unmet by existing structures" argument. This is not individual failure; it is the **designed output of the incentive system.**

- **Software isn't a first-class deliverable, so it stays a prototype.** Tools remain in a prototype state, or lose their maintenance incentives when project funding ends. *This is the mechanism that produces the thousand sloppy grad-student codebases.*
- **Funding ends; maintenance doesn't.** Tool funding is tied to a grant's duration; maintenance obligations extend far beyond it. Science has been "too singularly focused on innovation" to treat infrastructure maintenance as anything but a lesser task.
- **The incentive system punishes the work.** Promotion rides on metrics like the h-index; research-software engineering isn't an accepted academic profession; most academic developers (PhD students/postdocs) get no training in sustainable software.
- **The "valley of death"** between a research prototype and a sustained capability is a recognized structural gap with no good grant instrument spanning it.

## Why it *is* the X-Labs thesis
A calibrated, multimodal, semantically-unified capture platform is exactly the class of artifact the normal R01/standard-grant machine cannot produce: it needs full-time engineering, indefinite maintenance, cross-modality integration no single PI scopes, and an institutional home with autonomy and IP control. **The autonomy / full-time / IP-ownership requirements of X-Labs are the literal solution to the documented reason this class of platform doesn't get built.** `#reframe`
