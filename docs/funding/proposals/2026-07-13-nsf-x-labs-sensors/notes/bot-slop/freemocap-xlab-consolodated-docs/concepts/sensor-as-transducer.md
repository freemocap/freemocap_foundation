# Sensor as Transducer

A **transducer** converts one form of energy into another; a **sensor** is a transducer whose job is to convert a physical environmental quantity ([[eaee|EAEE]] — heat, pressure, light, motion, position) into an interpretable, almost always electrical, signal. This is **the hard-science floor** of the instrument.

> The boundary between the world and our system is **not a software API — it is a physical energy conversion.**

- A camera pixel transduces photon flux → charge.
- A load cell transduces force → voltage.
- An electrode transduces transmembrane ionic current → recordable potential.
- An IMU transduces inertial force → digital rate.

The instrument is an **array of transducers**, each converting a distinct band of EAEE into a repeatable pattern; everything downstream is the disciplined interpretation of those patterns. *That is the definition of an instrument.*

## The deep parallel that makes it perceptual-motor science
Nervous systems, at their sensory surface, are **exactly this** — arrays of transducers of environmental energy (photoreceptors, hair cells; see [[gibson-ecological-optics]]). So studying how animals control action on sensed information *is* an instrumentation problem: you must measure the same environmental energy the animal samples, and the animal's sampling of it (gaze, pose, force), **simultaneously and commensurably**. Not a metaphor — the literal experimental requirement.
