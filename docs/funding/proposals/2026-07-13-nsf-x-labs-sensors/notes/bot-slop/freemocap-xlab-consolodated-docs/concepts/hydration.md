# Hydration

**Hydration** is the coinage for *filling in a slot of the canonical [[ontology-definition|ontology]] with a grounded measurement.* The ontology exists as a **scaffold independent of any given measurement**; a measurement *hydrates* the part of it that the measurement touches. Unmeasured parts are **presumed present but un-hydrated** — structurally there, waiting to be grounded, but "we can't say anything about them yet."

> *"Heads have faces and faces have eyes even if you don't measure them."*

## The productive inverse of override
Hydration is the positive complement to the **override** logic of [[composition-over-inheritance]]:
- **Override** *removes/replaces* an inherited default (cavefish lose their eyes).
- **Hydration** *fills in* a default with grounded values as sensors are added.

## Two orthogonal axes (worth stating cleanly)
- **Coverage** — *which* parts of the scaffold are grounded at all (hydration).
- **Precision / layers of precision** — *how finely* a grounded part is resolved (an eye as a 6-DOF rigid body vs. a soft deformable musculoskeletal structure).

## The retroactive-improvement payoff
Because the scaffold persists, **the archive improves as the instrument improves**: once a better sensor exists (e.g. an [[next-generation-eye-trackers|eye tracker]] that reaches ocular torsion / lens accommodation via [[dual-purkinje-image|Purkinje imaging]]), models can **back-predict** those newly-hydratable slots for older recordings. A strong data-longevity argument. `#say`
