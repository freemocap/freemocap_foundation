# Daley & Biewener (2006) — Running Over Rough Terrain
**Citation:** Daley, M. A., & Biewener, A. A. (2006). Running over rough terrain reveals limb control for intrinsic stability. *PNAS* 103(42), 15681–15686. `@daley2006` · doi:10.1073/pnas.0601473103 · [PMC1622881](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1622881/) · *(web-verified this session; **not yet in `FMCF-NSF-XLABS.bib`**)*

**What it is:** Guinea fowl maintain dynamic stability through a large, unexpected drop in substrate height during running — evidence that limb mechanics provide **intrinsic stability** without requiring neural correction on the fast timescale.

**Why we cite it:** Grounds the **guinea fowl** as an animal-model node ([[comparative-phylogeny-and-ecs]]): an **obligate biped** whose musculoskeletal control gives a second, independent instance of bipedal locomotion to compare against humans — [[shared-vs-unshared-traits|shared trait, unshared lineage]], the [[composition-over-inheritance|component-ontology]] argument. M. Daley's model system.
