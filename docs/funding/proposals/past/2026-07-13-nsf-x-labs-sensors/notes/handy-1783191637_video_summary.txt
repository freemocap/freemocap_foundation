## Complete Topic Outline
### Research Framework Overview
> Introduction to a conceptual framework for scientific research, outlining core components from the real world to data generation and ontological structures.
- Real World & Desiderata
  - The 'Real World' (RW) contains the 'desiderata' (desired facts) or 'true facts' of the universe.
  - Desiderata are the true values associated with downstream estimates.

- Empirically Available Environmental Energy (EAEE)
  - RW is suffused with EAEE, an energy form for empirical observation.
  - Analogous to Gibsonian optic array, but explicitly framed for empirical availability in sensor-grounded ontologies.

### Sensor-Based Data Acquisition
> Methods and technologies used to capture and convert environmental energy into measurable data for research.
- Camera-Based Motion Capture (Mocap)
  - Examples focus on light as EAEE interacting with a camera sensor.
  - Camera transduces light into electrical energy, converted to video.
  - Modern convolutional neural networks/transformers analyze video to estimate key point trajectories and reconstruct skeletons for full body kinematics ('free mocap').
  - Characterized by low precision, high accuracy (noisy estimate around true answer).

- IMU-Based Motion Capture
  - Alternative method using IMU-based motion capture suits.
  - Measures linear and angular acceleration via accelerometers, gyroscopes, and magnetometers.
  - Reconstructs kinematics by measuring orientation of each body segment.
  - Characterized by low accuracy, high precision (high precision but prone to drift from true answer).

- Data Transformation and Estimation
  - Camera Mocap: starts with position, requires double differentiation for acceleration.
  - IMU Mocap: starts with acceleration, requires double integration for position.
  - Both yield similar estimates externally, suggesting a hybrid approach can correct for each other's errors.

### Sensor-Grounded Ontology
> Developing a structured understanding of entities and their properties based on sensor-derived measurements.
- Data Model Consistency
  - Camera, IMU, and hybrid mocap all produce the same data model.
  - Measurements used to estimate the position of canonical ontological entities.

- Hierarchical Entity-Component-Property Traits
  - Example: 'human' as a canonical entity.
  - Traits derived from phylogenetic tree (chordata, craniata, tetraprods, bipeds, primates).
  - Body segments, skeleton, hands (joints), head (face, eyes, pupils, lenses, retina, 3D orientation space).
  - These are layers of precision within the ontology; all are objects.

- Optical Sensors: Biological vs. Artificial
  - Eyes are optical sensors (pupil, lens, retina, focal length, projection, sensor).
  - Optical sensors can be eyes or cameras, sharing functional relationships.
  - Biological eyes have unique properties, but both interact with light (EAEE).

- Unmeasured Ontological Properties
  - Muscles and muscle activity are presumed to exist in human ontology even if not directly measured by sensors.
  - Estimation of muscle forces can be done with inverse dynamics (e.g., OpenSim) from kinematics.
  - Absence of direct sensor grounding means 'we can't say anything about them', but they are still part of the ontology.

- Sensor Grounding and Uncertainty
  - Estimates like muscle activity from inverse dynamics are grounded back to primary sensors (e.g., camera) through metrology, NIST calibration, and uncertainty propagation.
  - Muscle activity can also be directly measured with surface or implanted electrodes, providing additional layers of estimation power.

### Applications and Comparative Research
> How this framework supports interdisciplinary research, particularly between biology and artificial intelligence, and facilitates comparative studies.
- Interfacing with AI and Robotics
  - Ontological data grounds empirical measurements in instrumentation and energy.
  - Provides opportunities for interfacing with research on artificial agents and robotics.
  - Deeply grounded data serves as useful training for reinforcement learning or trajectory optimization.
  - Extracted theoretical control policies are testable on humans and other animals.

- Cross-Species and Cross-System Comparison
  - Relationship between different instantiations of the same thing.
  - Research applicability to humans is inversely related to shared/unshared traits.
  - More shared traits yield mechanistic insights (e.g., primate eyeballs for human eyeballs).
  - Studying diverse systems (e.g., bird/bee vision) provides functionalist insights (different machinery solving same problems).

- Biological vs. Artificial Agents
  - Biological eyes and cameras are mechanically different but functionally similar as optical sensors interacting with EAEE.
  - This functional similarity allows them to operate in a similar space within a functional, operational, sensor-grounded ontological research approach.
