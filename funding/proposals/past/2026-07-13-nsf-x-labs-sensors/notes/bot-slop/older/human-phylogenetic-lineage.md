# Human Phylogenetic Lineage — Animalia → *Homo sapiens*

> Companion reference to `human-lineage-tree.html`. Abbreviated tree showing the direct lineage path with the key clades highlighted. Dates are approximate (mya = million years ago).

```

 └─ Eumetazoa (~580) — true tissues, distinct germ layers (endoderm + ectoderm)
     └─ Bilateria (~570) — bilateral symmetry, cephalization, mesoderm, coelom
         └─ Deuterostomia (~550) — blastopore → anus, enterocoelic coelom
             └─ ★ CHORDATA (~540) — notochord + dorsal hollow nerve cord + pharyngeal slits + post-anal tail

                         └─ ★ VERTEBRATA (~525) — true vertebrae, segmented spinal column
                             └─ Gnathostomata (~460) — articulated jaws + paired fins + adaptive immune system
                                 └─ ★ OSTEICHTHYES (~420) — mineralized bone (hydroxyapatite), dermal bone, lungs
                                     └─ Sarcopterygii (~415) — lobe-fins, central bony axis, enamel teeth
                                         └─ ★ TETRAPODA (~390) — four weight-bearing limbs with digits + neck + sacrum
                                             └─ Amniota (~320) — amniotic egg (amnion, chorion, allantois)
                                                 └─ Synapsida (~310) — single temporal fenestra, differentiated teeth
                                                     └─ Therapsida (~275) — erect limb posture, heterodont dentition, partial 2° palate
                                                         └─ Cynodontia (~260) — multicusped molars, complete 2° palate, turbinates, probable fur
                                                             └─ ★ MAMMALIA (~225 stem / ~170 crown) — milk + fur + endothermy + 3 ear bones + neocortex
                                                                 └─ Theria (~160) — live birth, tribosphenic molars, nipples
                                                                     └─ Eutheria (~100) — chorioallantoic placenta, corpus callosum
                                                                         └─ Euarchontoglires (~85) — Euarchonta + Glires supraordinal clade
                                                                             └─ ★ PRIMATES (~65) — opposable hallux + pollex, nails, postorbital bar, expanded brain
                                                                                 └─ Haplorhini (~58) — dry rhinarium, retinal fovea, postorbital septum, no vitamin C
                                                                                     └─ Simiiformes (~45) — complete postorbital closure, trichromacy, fused jaw
                                                                                         └─ Catarrhini (~35) — downward nostrils, 2.1.2.3 dental formula

                                                                                                                 └─ ★ Homo sapiens (~0.3) — chin, globular braincase, symbolic thought, cumulative culture
```

## Key Clades — What Each Node Contributed to Human Anatomy

### Chordata (~540 mya)
**Innovation:** Notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, post-anal tail.

The four chordate synapomorphies, present at some developmental stage in all members. The notochord is a flexible rod that in vertebrates is largely replaced by the vertebral column. The dorsal hollow nerve cord becomes the brain and spinal cord. Human embryos still briefly form pharyngeal arches and a post-anal tail.

**Sister/outside:** Non-vertebrate chordates (lancelets, tunicates). Echinoderms (starfish) are the closest non-chordate deuterostomes.

---

### Craniata (~530 mya)
**Innovation:** Cranium (cartilaginous or bony brain case) + neural crest cells.

A protective brain case surrounding the brain and sensory capsules. Neural crest cells — sometimes called the "fourth germ layer" — migrate during development to form the face, jaw, sensory placodes, and much of the peripheral nervous system. Hagfish (Myxiniformes) are craniates: they have a cartilaginous skull but lack true vertebrae.

**Sister/outside:** Craniata = hagfish + Vertebrata. The hagfish lineage branches off here — they are craniates but may not be true vertebrates (whether they ancestrally never had vertebrae or secondarily lost them is still debated).

---

### Vertebrata (~525 mya)
**Innovation:** True vertebrae — segmented spinal column of cartilage or bone.

Segmented skeletal elements (centra, neural arches) surrounding and protecting the dorsal nerve cord. This is the backbone proper. In humans, the notochord is largely replaced by the vertebral column during development, persisting only as the nucleus pulposus of intervertebral discs. Lampreys (Petromyzontiformes) have rudimentary cartilaginous vertebrae and are the earliest-branching living vertebrates.

**Sister/outside:** Vertebrata = lampreys + Gnathostomata. The lamprey lineage branches off here. Our lineage continues through Gnathostomata (jawed vertebrates).

---

### Osteichthyes (~420 mya)
**Innovation:** Endochondral (bony) skeleton — **mineralized bone.**

The "bony fish" clade. The skeleton mineralizes with calcium phosphate (hydroxyapatite), replacing the cartilaginous model of earlier vertebrates. **Every bone in your body traces its evolutionary origin to this node.** Dermal bone (skull plates, clavicle) also appears here.

**Sister/outside:** Chondrichthyes (sharks, rays, skates) — cartilaginous skeleton only, no bone.

---

### Tetrapoda (~390 mya)
**Innovation:** Four weight-bearing limbs with digits (pentadactyl pattern).

The water-to-land transition. Lobe-fins become limbs with wrist, ankle, and digit joints. The five-digit pattern is the ancestral condition — whale flippers, bat wings, and horse hooves are all modified pentadactyl limbs. Neck, sacrum, and weight-bearing limb girdles appear.

**Sister/outside:** Lungfish (Dipnoi) are the closest living non-tetrapod relatives. Fossil taxa like *Tiktaalik* show the intermediate fin-to-limb transition.

---

### Amniota (~320 mya)
**Innovation:** Amniotic egg — reproduce away from water.

The amniotic egg (amnion, chorion, allantois) allows embryonic development on dry land, breaking the final tether to aquatic reproduction. Keratinized skin reduces water loss. The amniote lineage splits almost immediately into Sauropsida (reptiles + birds) and Synapsida (mammals).

**Sister/outside:** Amphibians (frogs, salamanders) must return to water to reproduce.

---

### Mammalia (~225 stem / ~170 crown mya)
**Innovation:** Milk (lactation) + fur + endothermy + three middle-ear bones + neocortex.

Lactation via mammary glands is the defining feature. Three middle-ear ossicles (malleus, incus, stapes — derived from former jaw bones) enable high-frequency hearing. Endothermy with fur insulation enables nocturnal and high-latitude activity. A six-layered neocortex supports complex sensory processing.

**Sister/outside:** Monotremes (platypus, echidna) are the earliest-branching living mammals — they lay eggs but produce milk.

---

### Primates (~65 mya)
**Innovation:** Opposable hallux + pollex with nails (not claws) + postorbital bar + expanded brain.

Nails replacing claws on grasping digits with an opposable hallux (big toe) and pollex (thumb) — the **grasping foot** is the key primate innovation; many arboreal mammals grasp with hands but primates specialize the foot for prehension. A bony postorbital bar encloses the lateral eye socket, providing rigidity for high-acuity depth discrimination (binocular vision itself is ancient — present since the origin of paired vertebrate eyes in early craniates). Olfactory apparatus reduced; visual cortex expanded disproportionately. Increased encephalization quotient (brain:body ratio) relative to other mammals.

**Sister/outside:** Colugos (flying lemurs) and treeshrews. Within Primates, lemurs and lorises (Strepsirrhini) branch off before the haplorhine line that leads to us.

---

### Hominidae (~15 mya)
**Innovation:** Great ape body plan — large body, no tail, complex social cognition, tool use.

Large body size, extended juvenile period with prolonged maternal care, complex social structures with theory of mind, and sophisticated tool use and manufacture. von Economo (spindle) neurons associated with social cognition appear in the great ape brain.

**Sister/outside:** Gibbons (Hylobatidae). Within Hominidae: orangutans split first (~15 mya), then gorillas (~8 mya), then the human-chimp clade.

---

### Homo (~2.8 mya)
**Innovation:** Obligate bipedalism + massive brain expansion + stone tool manufacture + language.

Obligate upright walking with a fully adducted hallux, pelvis reshaped for bipedal locomotion, and lumbar lordosis. Brain volume expands from ~400 cc (australopiths) to ~1,350 cc (*H. sapiens*). Oldowan stone tools appear ~2.6 mya. Controlled fire ~1.5 mya with *H. erectus*. Language — with the descended larynx and FOXP2 gene modifications — likely emerges within *Homo*.

**Sister/outside:** *Australopithecus* (including Lucy — *A. afarensis*, ~3.2 mya) and *Paranthropus*. Within *Homo*: *H. habilis*, *H. erectus*, *H. neanderthalensis*, *H. denisova* — all extinct except us.

---

### *Homo sapiens* (~0.3 mya)
**Innovation:** Anatomically modern humans — chin, globular braincase, symbolic thought, cumulative culture.

Appears in Africa ~300 kya. Prominent chin, globular braincase, gracile skeleton. Behavioral modernity — symbolic art, burial rituals, long-distance trade, cumulative cultural evolution — appears ~100–50 kya. Sole surviving hominin species.

**Sister/outside:** *H. neanderthalensis* (LCA ~600–500 kya; we carry ~1–2% Neanderthal DNA). *H. denisova* (Melanesians carry ~3–5% Denisovan DNA).
