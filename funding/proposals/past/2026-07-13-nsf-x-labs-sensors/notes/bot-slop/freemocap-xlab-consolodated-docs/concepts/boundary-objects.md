# Boundary Objects (build for a student, get a commons)

A design lesson from FreeMoCap: **build with a hypothetical student in mind and the tool becomes a cross-disciplinary commons.** The science-studies names:

- **Boundary objects** (Star & Griesemer, 1989): artifacts "plastic enough to adapt to local needs, yet robust enough to maintain a common identity across sites" — what lets people from different worlds coordinate without a shared discipline.
- **Trading zones** (Galison): interactional spaces where different expert cultures build a shared "pidgin."

## The key insight `#design-lesson`
**Skipping "step zero"** — assuming everyone took the same prerequisite — doesn't just exclude novices; it **freezes out experts from *other* disciplines** (the curse of knowledge). A tool that never skips step zero is therefore not dumbed-down; it is the **only** kind a physicist, a biologist, and a roboticist can all enter. *Designing for the student is designing the boundary object* — structural value, not just outreach.

This is JSM's outline vision: researchers "come together to discuss the tool itself... they'll talk about the confusing implications of the names of menus," growing "a new culture of science focused on collaborative development of shared tools rather than an endless parade of 8–10 page PDFs."
