# Shared vs. Unshared Traits (the comparative axis)

The scientific-payoff structure that turns "we study lots of animals" into a **principled comparative design**:

- **Shared traits → mechanistic insight.** The closer an organism's machinery is to ours (primate eyeballs ≈ human eyeballs), the more directly its mechanisms tell us how *our* system works. *"How does OUR system work?"*
- **Unshared traits → functionalist insight.** The farther away (bird / bee / octopus vision), the more it reveals about *how the same task is solved with different machinery.* *"How do you solve an equivalent problem differently?"*

## The sharp point: trait-overlap ≠ lineage `#design-lesson`
The **ferret** is phylogenetically *farther* from humans than a mouse (Laurasiatheria vs. our Euarchontoglires), yet its visual system is *more human-like* — frontal-ish predator eyes, binocular overlap, **orientation columns in V1** (like primates), where mouse/rat V1 is non-columnar "salt-and-pepper." **Convergence** means a [[entity-component-system|component/trait ontology]] captures "what maps to humans" **better than the inheritance tree does.** This is the biological vindication of [[composition-over-inheritance]].
