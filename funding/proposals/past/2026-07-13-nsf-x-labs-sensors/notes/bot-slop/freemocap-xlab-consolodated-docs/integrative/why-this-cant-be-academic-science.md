# Why This Can't Be Academic Science (unmet by existing structures)

This is the section that does the solicitation's required **"unmet by existing structures and funding mechanisms"** work — and it maps almost verbatim onto the X-Labs **dual mandate** (§3.0): *Build Sector-Defining Platforms* ("beyond traditional outputs like publications and datasets") and *Develop New Organizational Research Structures* ("challenges that lack sufficient incentives within traditional academic and industry labs"). Both objectives are, nearly word-for-word, arguments *against academia doing this.*

## The incentive-incompatibility argument (it's lawful, not a skill gap)
JSM's intuitions map onto named, cited results:
- **C1 — Novelty bias.** Science rewards being *first*, not *useful* or *durable*; an integrative instrument that *subsumes* [[markerless-pose-estimation|DeepLabCut]] as one component reads as "not new" to a novelty-gated reviewer. The incentive system literally cannot see integration's value.
- **C2 — [[research-software-sustainability|Research software becomes abandonware]]** — documented, not anecdotal: maintenance isn't rewarded, funding flows to new projects, RSEs leave for industry.
- **C3 — Software rot is a law** ([[path-dependence|Lehman's laws]]; Cunningham's technical debt; Foote & Yoder's Big Ball of Mud). "Rome built on Rome." Academia supplies the forces and none of the paydown.
- **C4 — Step-zero lock-in is [[path-dependence|path dependence]]** (David/QWERTY; Arthur/lock-in): low-information early decisions define the maximum complexity a project can ever handle.
- **C5 — The blank-page refactor** is engineering wisdom (Brooks: "plan to throw one away") but career-suicidal in academia. Its main danger (Spolsky) is a *market* penalty — which **doesn't apply to precompetitive science**. The objection becomes the justification.
- **C6 — [[conways-law|Conway's Law]] is the linchpin:** architecture mirrors the org's communication structure, so academia produces abandonware-big-balls-of-mud *by law*. Fixing the instrument requires fixing the **organization** first — the literal reason the solicitation pairs "platform technology" with "new organizational structure." An X-Lab is the **Inverse Conway Maneuver.**

> **The synthesis `#reframe`:** the X-Labs structure — sustained multi-year funding, operational autonomy, freedom from publication metrics, and organizational independence — is the *specific, minimal intervention that removes every one of these constraints at once.*

## The two sanctioned precedents `#precedent`
- **[[obo-foundry|OBO/Gene Ontology]]** — the *organizational* template (a sustained, federated, governed consortium stewarding precompetitive infrastructure). Its **~25-year maturation is the argument, not an embarrassment**: durable shared infrastructure has a decades-long horizon and survives only on sustained non-market stewardship — exactly the X-Labs funding shape (Phase 0 → Phase 1 ≤$50M/yr → Phase 2 → possible Phase 3).
- **The Internet** — named by the solicitation *itself* (§7.0). The [[narrow-waist|narrow-waist]] architecture, NSF's own gap-filling role (**NSFNET, 1986–1995**), and "rough consensus and running code" (build-to-spec, then standardize) are all on-message via the program's own reference class.

## How it lasts — dissemination & governance
The org argument only works if the thing *endures and spreads*. [[tiered-dissemination-and-communal-science|Tiered dissemination]] (Tier-0 flagship → partner labs → universities → classrooms, all on one [[metrology-and-traceability|traceable]] ontology) + **home-lab publisher/subscriber** release (no mandated proprietary window) + the [[conceptual-cleanliness-four-audiences|four-audiences]] force-multiplier + [[bdfl-governance|BDfaSPoT governance]] toward a **federation** (Eghbal), designed against the **stadium**. And measurement-first, federated, community-owned = the **[[tiered-dissemination-and-communal-science|anti–Human Brain Project]]** `#cautionary-tale`, our answer to any "overreach" worry.
