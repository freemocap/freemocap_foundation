# Yates et al. (2025) — OpenIrisDPI
**Citation:** Yates, J. L., et al. (2025). OpenIrisDPI: An Open-Source Digital Dual Purkinje Image Eye Tracker for Visual Neuroscience. *bioRxiv* 2025.04.18.649589. `@yates2025` · [bioRxiv v2](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.04.18.649589v2) · [PMC12697258](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12697258/) · *(web-verified this session; **not yet in `FMCF-NSF-XLABS.bib`** — add on JSM approval)*

**What it is:** An open-source plugin (for the OpenIris framework) implementing **dual-Purkinje-image** tracking on **consumer-grade cameras** — binocular tracking of Purkinje reflections + pupil simultaneously at **500 Hz**. Reports the DPI signal as markedly less variable than pupil–corneal-reflection (model-eye DPI RMS 0.27′ vs. P-CR 1.83′).

**Why we cite it:** This is the "**DPI with regular cameras**" result JSM referenced. It is the direct evidence for the **smartphone-camera thesis** ([[next-generation-eye-trackers]]): commodity imaging is now good enough to recover [[dual-purkinje-image|Purkinje signals]] (→ lens/accommodation), so the eye-tracker build is an engineering-packaging problem, not a physics problem. Grounds a concrete Phase-1 hardware deliverable.
