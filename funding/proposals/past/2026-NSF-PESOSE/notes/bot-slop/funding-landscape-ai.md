## The reframe that matters most

**FreeMoCap and SkellyBot have opposite problems, and they are not fundable by the same
mechanisms.**

| | FreeMoCap | SkellyBot |
|---|---|---|
| Adoption | large, growing | classroom deployments, never spread |
| Community | real users, thin contributors | essentially none |
| What's missing | organization, governance, sustainability | evidence and users |

Nearly every open-source sustainability funder gates on **demonstrated adoption**. The
Open Source for Science Fund states the exclusion verbatim — "an early-stage software
prototype that is not yet used extensively beyond its creator(s) or a narrow community of
users" is explicitly out of scope. PESOSE says the same thing differently ("not intended
to fund the development of open-source artifacts").

So **SkellyBot is not fundable as an open-source sustainability project.** Pitching it that
way will fail on eligibility at nearly every door in this document.

It *is* potentially fundable as **education research**, where the deliverable is evidence
about learning rather than a sustained tool. Prior classroom deployment across multiple
universities is an asset in that frame and a liability in the OSS frame. Different funders,
different claim, different reviewers.

## Tier 1 — worth acting on

### Sloan Foundation — Open Source in Science
**Verified: primary source fetched.** https://sloan.org/programs/digital-technology/open-source-in-science

- **Rolling two-page letter of inquiry. No deadline.** Lowest-cost shot in this document.
- Program goal: "tools, norms, and institutions that take full advantage of open source
  practices to support the distributed development, adoption, and maintenance of
  discovery-enabling software, hardware, and models."
- **Critical caveat, verbatim:** "Rather than funding individual open source development
  projects, grants in this area focus mainly on tooling, institutions, economic models,
  and incentives." FreeMoCap-as-a-tool is not the pitch. FreeMoCap-as-a-case-study in
  governance and sustainability models is.
- **The AI hook is already in their focus areas.** Under "Roles & Career Paths": "at a time
  when AI and other automation tools are likely to reshape the division of labor engaged in
  such work, this program aims to develop optimal models for **human-AI collaboration in
  scientific software practices**." That is a direct opening for the AI-plus-open-source
  interest, and it is a better-aimed door than the PESOSE DCL.
- Program Director: Joshua M. Greenberg.

### Open Source for Science Fund — successor to CZI EOSS
**Verified: primary sources fetched.** https://os4science.org

- New multi-donor fund at Renaissance Philanthropy, launched **May 2026**, $20M seeded by
  Biohub and Wellcome, plus Kavli and ReSA. Built by the team behind CZI EOSS ($58M across
  230+ projects over six cycles).
- **The inaugural call (OS4LS) is CLOSED.** LOIs closed 8 Jun 2026; full applications
  closed 21 Jul 2026; notifications October 2026. Missed by weeks.
- **Watch for the next call.** Terms of the first one: Track 1 up to $250K/2yr for
  domain-specific tools; Track 2 up to $1M/2yr for foundational libraries and ecosystem
  initiatives. Indirect capped at 10%.
- **Eligibility fits.** Nonprofits eligible; projects without an institutional home may use
  a fiscal sponsor, with **NumFOCUS and Code for Science & Society named explicitly**.
- **Life sciences scoped — but neuroscience is listed in scope** under translational /
  application areas. Whether markerless motion capture for motor neuroscience and
  biomechanics reads as life sciences to their reviewers is a real question worth asking
  info@os4science.org directly, well before the next call.
- **The AI framing they want is the one considered for PESOSE.** Priority given to
  "protocols that unlock the use of open source tools in agentic workflows" and
  "interoperability frameworks that make tools composable in AI-driven pipelines." If the
  agent-interoperability idea has legs, this is a better-aimed venue than DCL 26-015 —
  same idea, funder actively asking for it, no requirement to bolt it onto a governance
  proposal.

## Tier 2 — plausible, needs verification

### NSF CSSI — Cyberinfrastructure for Sustained Scientific Innovation
**Verified: program page fetched. Deadline NOT established.**
https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/cssi-cyberinfrastructure-sustained-scientific-innovation

- Still on solicitation **NSF 22-632**; page last updated 9 Jan 2026. No upcoming due date
  was listed on the page as of this scan — may be between cycles. **Verify before planning
  around it.**
- Three classes: Elements (small, ~20 awards), Framework Implementations (~10),
  **Transition to Sustainability** (~5, up to $4M available) — the third is aimed at
  "groups who would like to execute a well-defined sustainability plan for existing CI
  with demonstrated impact," which is close to the FreeMoCap situation.
- **Hard constraint: one proposal per person, period.** An individual may be PI, co-PI, or
  Senior Personnel on **at most one** proposal across all three classes. Anyone whose
  biosketch appears counts, funded or not. Violations returned without review, "no
  exceptions."
- Eligibility for a standalone nonprofit lead needs checking against the solicitation.

### NSF EducateAI → IUSE: CUE
**Verified: search only; primary solicitation not fetched.**
DCL: https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/dcl-advancing-education-future-ai-workforce-educateai/nsf24-025 ·
IUSE:CUE: NSF 24-553

- EducateAI is a **DCL, not a competition** — same structural point as the PESOSE AI DCL.
  It routes proposals into IUSE: CUE and related programs.
- Closest federal fit for the SkellyBot work: AI-first STEM teaching, undergraduate,
  evidence-generating.
- Nonprofit non-academic organizations are an eligible category, but IUSE is built around
  institutions of higher education. **Realistically this needs a university partner as lead
  or co-lead**, which given prior deployments at multiple universities may be the natural
  path anyway.

### NSF AISL — Advancing Informal STEM Learning (NSF 24-601)
**Verified: search only. Deadline NOT established.**

- Nonprofits are described as core to the program, not merely tolerated — one of the few
  NSF programs genuinely designed around non-academic nonprofit leads.
- Fit depends on whether the work is framed as informal/lifelong learning rather than
  classroom instruction. FreeMoCap's educational reach may fit better here than SkellyBot's
  classroom deployments do.

## Tier 3 — watch, low confidence

Everything below is from **aggregator sites, not funder primary sources.** Treat as leads
to verify, not facts.

- **Humanity AI** — $500M over 5 years, ten foundations (MacArthur, Ford, Mellon, Lumina,
  Kapor, Mozilla, Omidyar, Doris Duke, Packard, Siegel), **education a named priority**,
  grants beginning 2026 via Rockefeller Philanthropy Advisors. Largest AI-and-education
  pool identified. Verify whether it takes unsolicited proposals.
- **Schmidt Sciences** — AI2050 Fellows; HAVI (humanities + AI); Science of Trustworthy AI.
  None is an obvious fit for education tools or motion capture.
- **Mozilla Foundation** — Fellows (~$100K) and AI & Democracy awards. Framing is
  information ecosystems and algorithmic accountability; weak fit.
- **NumFOCUS** — already used for a small dev grant. Relevant less as a funder than as the
  **fiscal sponsor / intermediary NSF itself recommends** (see the PESOSE "don't go it
  alone" note) and that OS4LS names as an acceptable sponsor. That dual role makes a
  NumFOCUS conversation useful across several of these doors at once.

## NIH — probably not

**Verified: search only; all SEPA announcements found were expired.**

NIH is a poor fit for both projects unless there is a genuine biomedical claim, and the
PESOSE Q&A webinar flagged the inverse risk too — framing work as a medical solution makes
an NSF proposal read like an NIH submission.

The one mechanism worth knowing about is **SEPA (Science Education Partnership Award, R25)**
— nonprofits with 501(c)(3) status are explicitly eligible, including science centers and
museums. But it is scoped to **pre-K–12** students and teachers plus informal science
education, not undergraduate. The most recent announcement located (PAR-23-137) is expired;
**current status unverified.** If the education interest ever points at K-12 rather than
university classrooms, this becomes worth a real look.

## Suggested sequencing

1. **Sloan LOI** — two pages, no deadline, no institutional sign-off. Highest
   value-per-hour item on this list, and it can go out without disturbing the PESOSE work.
2. **Email info@os4science.org** — ask about life-sciences scope for motor neuroscience /
   biomechanics tooling, and when the next call opens. Costs nothing, resolves the largest
   open question about the best-fit funder found in this scan.
3. **Verify CSSI's current cycle** before assuming it exists. Note the one-proposal-per-
   person rule if any CSSI submission is contemplated alongside others.
4. **Decide SkellyBot's frame** — education research, not open-source sustainability. That
   decision determines which of the remaining doors is even worth knocking on.

## Caveats on this scan

- Deadlines for CSSI, IUSE:CUE, AISL and SEPA were **not established**. Several NSF
  programs appear to be running on older solicitations with uncertain next cycles.
- Tier 3 rests on aggregator summaries. Verify at the funder before relying on any figure.
- This scan did not cover: IES (Dept. of Education), DoD education/STEM programs, NEH,
  state-level funders, corporate philanthropy (Google.org, Microsoft AI for Good), or
  accelerator/fellowship routes.
