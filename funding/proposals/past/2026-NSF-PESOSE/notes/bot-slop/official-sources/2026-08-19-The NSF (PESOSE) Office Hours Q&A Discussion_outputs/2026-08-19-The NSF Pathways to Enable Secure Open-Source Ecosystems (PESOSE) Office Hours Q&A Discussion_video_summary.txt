# Webinar Q&A on PSOS Ecosystems Funding and Governance 

Title slug: webinar-q-a-on-psos-ecosystems-funding-and-governance


## One Sentence Summary
This webinar Q&A clarifies funding eligibility for PSOS ecosystems, distinguishes between R&D and ecosystem building, outlines requirements for open data sets and software, discusses FFRDC involvement, budgeting for I-Corp and hardware, and emphasizes governance and community engagement.

## Executive Summary
The webinar addresses key aspects of PSOS (Platform for Scientific Open Software) ecosystems, emphasizing that funding is for ecosystem building, not direct R&D. It clarifies requirements for open data sets and software systems in proposals, stressing intellectual merit and broader impacts. The discussion also covers the intricacies of FFRDC involvement, budgeting for hardware and I-Corp training, and the critical role of governance, community contributions, and mentorship in fostering sustainable open-source ecosystems. Participants are guided on effectively framing their technology and ecosystem maturity for NSF funding.

## Complete Topic Outline
### PSOS Funding Scope and Eligibility
> Clarification on what activities are funded under the PSOS program, distinguishing between R&D and ecosystem building, and guidelines for participant support and international engagements.
- R&D vs. Ecosystem Building
  - PSOS is not for Research and Development (R&D); direct R&D activities are not funded.
  - Activities like building hardware or hackathons are acceptable if framed as 'building the ecosystem' or 'engagement'.

- Workshop Subsidies and Participant Support
  - Funds can be used to subsidize hardware costs for workshops and researcher travel.
  - Participant support is permissible under NSF clearance.

- International Engagements
  - NSF funds cannot support international engagements, either for proposers or workshop participants.
  - Alternative mechanisms must be found to support international individuals.

### Proposal Requirements for Open Assets
> Guidelines for presenting open data sets and software systems in proposals, focusing on intellectual merit, broader impacts, and ecosystem development.
- Open Data Sets
  - Proposals are required to include a link to the open data set asset.
  - Data sets alone are not sufficient; intellectual merit and broader impacts must be discussed.
  - Proposers need to explain how the data set advances knowledge, its uniqueness, and the technology communities it serves.

- Open Software Systems (GitHub Repositories)
  - Multiple GitHub repositories are permissible, but the overall ecosystem must make sense to reviewers.
  - Focus is on advancing intellectual merit and broader impacts, not just the number of products.
  - Proposals must address the maturity of the ecosystem, including data maintainers, contributors, and governance structures.
  - Reviewers must be convinced of the synergy and strategy for managing multiple assets.

### FFRDC Involvement and Budgeting
> Considerations for involving Federally Funded Research and Development Centers (FFRDCs) and general budgeting advice for hardware and training.
- FFRDC Involvement in NSF Proposals
  - NSF cannot use funds to support something an FFRDC's institution or government agency is already funding.
  - Proposers must demonstrate why an FFRDC is needed and what special skill set they bring.
  - Need to explain how the FFRDC collaborators will be paid.
  - Send an email to the program with details about their participation for confirmation.

- Budgeting for Hardware and Training
  - Strong case needed for purchasing infrastructure/hardware.
  - Budgeting for I-Corp training is allowed, up to $30,000.
  - Additional compensation for participants in I-Corp training is permissible.
  - Training activities must be directly related to the project's objectives.
  - Caution against consuming the entire budget for I-Corp training.

- Project Budgeting and Compensation
  - Ensure all team members are compensated, not just PI and co-PIs.
  - Unequal compensation can create a 'negative look' on the budget.

### Ecosystem Definition and Maturity
> Guidelines for defining the scope and stakeholders of an open-source ecosystem, assessing its maturity, and the expectations for Track 1 and Track 2 projects.
- Defining the Ecosystem
  - Importance of defining who the target audience, contributors, and maintainers are.
  - Applicants must define and justify who they are trying to reach.
  - Size alone is not a good selling point; broader impacts are crucial.

- Open Source Ecosystem Maturity and Trajectory
  - Proposals should link to existing publicly available open-source products to show activity.
  - Proposers must articulate the maturity level of their ecosystem and its desired trajectory.
  - Simply making something 'bigger or more active' is not sufficient for intellectual merit.

- Project Proposal Maturity (Track 1 vs. Track 2)
  - Track 2 requires a governance structure, strategies for onboarding and growing maintainers and contributors.
  - Product maturity alone is insufficient for Track 2 if ecosystem development and governance are lacking.
  - Track 1 implies early stages of scoping and planning, even with existing users/contributors.

### I-Corps and Governance in Ecosystem Building
> Detailed expectations for I-Corps participation, funding for governance structures, and the role of mentorship programs in open-source ecosystems.
- I-Corps Participation Expectations
  - Track 2 applicants must discuss 'learnings' from I-Corps, not just activities.
  - Track 1 applicants should detail specific 'strategies for doing interviews' and workshop plans.
  - I-Corps is a structured learning activity for awardees to organize interactions and scope/plan their ecosystem.

- Funding Governance in Track 1 Projects
  - Track 1 is for 'scoping and planning and strategies,' not for making definitive decisions or implementing final structures.
  - Funds can be used to develop strategies and have dialogues with groups regarding governance.
  - Caution advised on direct legal fees due to restrictions in 2CFR 200.
  - Governance is broad, encompassing IP, decision-making, voting, and stakeholder involvement.

- Funding Mentorship Programs and Crowdfunding
  - Question about funding mentorships supported by crowdfunding platforms (e.g., Linux Foundation).
  - Need to convince reviewers of a direct link between crowdfunding and the applicant's ecosystem, ensuring oversight and control.

### Federal Agency Software and Open Source
> Challenges and considerations for transitioning scientific software maintained by a federal agency into a community-governed open-source project.
- Software Background and Current State
  - Scientific software developed and maintained by a federal agency, often with limited external contributors due to tight agency control.
  - Interest in evolving a community governance model for state agencies and private sector users.

- Red Flags and Dilemmas
  - Concerns about the software being 'locked down' by a federal agency.
  - Questioning the long-term vision and authority to move the software into a truly open-source state.
  - Dilemma: is it an ecosystem build or just fixing software for another federal agency?

- Path Forward
  - Forking the software as a potential solution to gain authority and enable development.
  - Continued concern about the lack of external code contributors for collaboration letters.

### Budgeting and I-Corps Training Specifics
> Detailed guidance on budget allocation for I-Corps, participant stipends, and travel within the program's financial framework.
- Budget Allocation Flexibility
  - The budget is flexible, and spending priorities are key, with I-Corps being one activity.
  - Participant support (stipends) is allowed.
  - Travel to conferences and for customer discovery is permitted.

- I-Corps Program Details
  - I-Corps involves three to five people (technical lead, entrepreneur lead, industry mentor).
  - Coursework is approximately seven weeks, one day per week.
  - Time to attend training is permitted.

- Budgetary Considerations for I-Corps
  - Clarification on whether I-Corp training counts towards the $30,000 limit.
  - Over half the money going towards I-Corps could be a problem for Track 1 proposals.
  - Approximately no more than a certain percentage should be focused solely on I-Corps activities.
