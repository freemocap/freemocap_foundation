### _21 What problem is your proposal addressing, and how does it affect the community you seek to serve in particular? Please identify the community or communities in your response. MAX 200 WORDS_

Access to powerful software and AI does not automatically provide the guidance people need to learn independently. Learners bring different goals, backgrounds, and levels of experience, while educators and community experts have limited time to provide individualized support. Many AI applications also give these communities little influence over what or how they teach.

[FreeMoCap](https://freemocap.org/) is free, open-source, AI-powered motion capture software that reconstructs 3D movement from camera recordings for animation, art, research, athletics, and education. Its international community includes more than 15,000 users across 152 countries. Users must learn camera setup, calibration, troubleshooting, and data interpretation, then apply these skills to their own projects.

Language learners need sustained conversational practice and feedback suited to their proficiency, interests, and language community. Teachers and speakers need to influence the content, linguistic conventions, and teaching behavior of the systems used for that practice.

We will develop SkellyBot for FreeMoCap users and SkellySpeak for language learners, investigating how community-guided AI teaching can support independent learning across these distinct contexts.

### _22 Provide an overview of the proposal approach. What outcome does it aim to achieve, and why do you believe this approach will be effective? MAX 200 WORDS_

Over two years, we will develop SkellyBot and SkellySpeak in parallel, using each community's needs to guide its application and the architecture they share.

SkellyBot will become a desktop learning companion integrated with FreeMoCap, our motion capture software. It will help users set up cameras, calibrate the capture system, troubleshoot problems, and understand their results. It will also explain the technical principles underlying these steps and help users apply the resulting data to their artistic, scientific, athletic, or educational projects.

[SkellySpeak](https://github.com/freemocap/skellyspeak) will provide conversational language practice, with speakers, teachers, and researchers shaping its linguistic foundations, learning content, and feedback.

Both applications will share internal AI dataflow graphs that organize information flow between AI operations, and a model-agnostic architecture supporting different AI models. Both will be free and open-source desktop applications, with interface design informed by community trials.

Development will draw on SkellyBot's classroom iterations and feedback from educators and learners connected to The Possible Zone and San José State University. Funding will support development, maintenance, and community design and evaluation. We will release both applications and reusable architectural components, and evaluate which components and teaching approaches transfer across learning contexts.

### _23 Specify the role of community design and leadership. How will the community or communities you seek to serve lead, shape, or participate in the work? MAX 150 WORDS_

The Possible Zone, a Boston nonprofit offering hands-on STEAM and career exploration for young people, brings experience supporting learners with varied technical backgrounds. David Bayus, Senior Lecturer in Digital Media Art at San José State University, already uses FreeMoCap to teach 3D art and animation.

Through these relationships, we will invite students and educators to identify setup difficulties, gaps in technical understanding, and connections to their own projects. Their priorities and trials will guide SkellyBot's teaching behavior and development roadmap.

For SkellySpeak, language speakers, teachers, and learners will guide conversational contexts, learning content, and feedback.

FreeMoCap's international community will participate through weekly calls, public Discord discussions, and open development. Participants will help assess usefulness and prioritize revisions, shaping both the shared architecture and the teaching approaches specific to each community.

### _24 Help us get to know you. What relevant background, experience, and/or relationships do you and any collaborators bring to this work? MAX 200 WORDS_

FreeMoCap Foundation is a 501(c)(3) nonprofit combining open-source software development, scientific research, and teaching. Founder Jonathan Samir Matthis developed and iteratively designed SkellyBot across six semesters of his Northeastern University course, "The Neural Control of Real-World Human Movement." Students discussed each lecture with the bot and connected the material to their own interests. Matthis found this enabled substantially more advanced undergraduate teaching while supporting individual exploration.

Our relationship with The Possible Zone has produced plans for a student workshop introducing motion capture, computer vision, and biomechanics, with learner and educator feedback guiding accessible STEAM activities and career exploration.

[David Bayus](https://cadre.sjsu.edu/faculty/david-bayus.html), Senior Lecturer in Digital Media Art at San José State University's CADRE Laboratory for New Media, has installed FreeMoCap in his classroom and developed an open-source Blender add-on connecting movement capture to animation.

Chief Scientific Officer Aaron Cherian contributes biomechanics, validation, and software expertise. Dr. [Conor Quinn](https://mila.quebec/en/ai4humanity/applied-projects/first-languages-ai-reality), co-founder and lead linguist of First Languages AI Reality (FLAIR), an Indigenous-led language revitalization initiative, will help shape SkellySpeak's linguistic architecture and facilitate connections with language communities.
