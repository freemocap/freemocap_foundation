# FreeMoCap Funding Opportunities to Track

## 1. Better Scientific Software (BSSw) Fellowship

**Link:** https://bssw.io/pages/apply-for-the-bssw-fellowship-program  
**Deadline:** October 30, 2026  
**Funding:** Up to $25,000  
**Status:** Open

### What it is
The Better Scientific Software Fellowship supports people who are improving the quality, sustainability, productivity, and broader practice of scientific software.

Fellows propose a funded activity that produces a publicly available artifact for the scientific-software community. Past/allowed activities include workshops, tutorials, educational resources, community-building activities, and other efforts that promote better scientific software practice.

### What they are looking for
They are looking for applicants who:
- Have meaningful experience with scientific software
- Have ideas, methods, tools, or practices that could improve scientific software broadly
- Can produce something useful to the larger scientific-software community rather than only benefiting their own project
- Can serve as advocates/leaders around better scientific software practices

Applicants must be affiliated with a U.S.-based institution capable of receiving federal funding.

### Potential FreeMoCap angle:
This grant is closer to the PESOSE grant, in that it doesn't support software development directly, but instead tools and resources around it. Scope of this might be artifacts for the educational part of FreeMoCap (like, something that breaks down exactly how markerless motion capture works). It could also be around the need for transparent validation practices and mechanisms as a whole - and how to work towards diagnostic improvements in software. Lots of ways to approach it.

## 2. DAPR Precision Rehabilitation Pilot Grant

**Link:** https://dapr.usc.edu/grants  
**LOI Deadline:** September 25, 2026  
**Full Proposal Deadline:** October 16, 2026  
**Funding:** Up to approximately $31,000 direct costs / $33,480 total  
**Status:** Open

### What it is
The Data Science and Analytics for Precision Rehabilitation (DAPR) Center funds pilot projects that advance the use of data science in rehabilitation research.

The broader goal of DAPR is to create large, harmonized rehabilitation datasets that can support artificial intelligence, machine learning, and precision-rehabilitation research.

### What they are looking for
The current call specifically emphasizes:

1. **Data management, sharing, and harmonization**
   - Especially harmonizing existing rehabilitation datasets into established standards

2. **Precision-rehabilitation algorithms and federated learning**
   - Development, evaluation, or application of algorithms for rehabilitation data
   - Methods that allow analysis across distributed datasets

Projects do not necessarily need to involve new clinical data collection. Existing datasets, data infrastructure, harmonization, and methodological work are explicitly relevant.

### Potential FreeMoCap angle
A particularly strong direction may be creating infrastructure for interoperable time-series movement data.

For example:
- Harmonizing markerless and marker-based motion-capture datasets
- Developing standards or schemas for gait and kinematic data
- Building translation/export tools between FreeMoCap outputs and rehabilitation data standards
- Demonstrating harmonization using existing FreeMoCap/Qualisys datasets and potentially an external rehabilitation dataset

This one is interesting, don't know if its the best fit - and I think requires some more effort than all the others here. But also has ESI considerations, so we would have some advantage if we applied for it

## 3. Humanity AI Open Call

**Link:** https://humanityai.ai/open-call/  
**Deadline:** October 21, 2026 at 7:59 PM ET  
**Funding:** $75,000–$1,000,000  
**Status:** Open

### What it is
Humanity AI is a major philanthropic initiative focused on ensuring that artificial intelligence serves the public good.

Its open call supports U.S.-based organizations and collaborations working with U.S. communities to influence how AI is governed, built, challenged, or used.

Lead applicants must generally be U.S.-based 501(c)(3) organizations.

### What they are looking for
They are not primarily looking for conventional AI R&D or generic "AI literacy" programs.

They are especially interested in projects where communities have meaningful power in shaping technology, including work involving:
- AI governance
- Community control and participation
- Economic opportunity
- Security and wellbeing
- Arts, culture, and creativity
- Public-interest technology
- Open-source/open-weight AI approaches

### Potential FreeMoCap angle
I would argue that this call might be better suited towards the other AI tools Jon has been building (like SkellyBot), rather than FreeMoCap. The general idea is in bringing AI to people. 

But if we wanted a FreeMoCap route, the framing would likely focus on how human-motion AI is designed and governed.

Potential themes include:
- Community-informed human-motion AI
- Pose-estimation systems that better represent disabled people, prosthesis users, atypical morphologies, or other populations poorly served by existing models
- Open and transparent alternatives to proprietary movement-analysis systems
- Giving communities more control over how movement data are collected, interpreted, and used

## 4. Sentient Foundation Open Source AGI Grant Program

**Link:** https://sentient.foundation/grants  
**Deadline:** Rolling — no fixed deadline  
**Funding:** Amounts are not fixed publicly; Sentient has committed $42 million across its broader grant/investment program  
**Status:** Open and reviewed on a rolling basis

### What it is
The Sentient Foundation funds developers, researchers, maintainers, and public-goods builders working on open-source AI.

The program is explicitly designed as an alternative to proprietary AI ecosystems. Grant recipients retain ownership of their work; Sentient does not take equity or impose lockups through the grant program.

### What they are looking for
They are particularly interested in:
- Open-source AI infrastructure
- Open models, code, data, evaluations, and tools
- Technology that can be used without dependence on a small number of proprietary AI companies
- Tools accessible to underserved communities and markets
- Open-source public goods
- Local or independently deployable AI systems

### Potential FreeMoCap angle

This is the weird one - the thing is that on paper, if it wasn't for the word AGI, we'd be exactly what they want. Everything on their website that describes what they're looking for points to us. So the trick is that if we apply to this, we'd need to do FreeMoCap + some AI integration. Something like, FreeMoCap + AI to help interpret or understand the results, or something. Especially if we describe FreeMoCap as something like open AI infrastructure or some buzzword like that. According to the website, they say the grant application does not actually take much time to fill out.

## 5. NLnet Restack — Open Internet Stack

**Link:** https://nlnet.nl/restack/  
**Deadline:** November 3, 2026 at 12:00 CET  
**Funding:** €5,000–€50,000 for a first proposal; successful projects may later scale substantially  
**Status:** Open

### What it is
Restack is an NLnet / European Commission-supported program intended to strengthen the **Open Internet Stack**.

It funds free and open-source technology that contributes to an open, resilient, interoperable, trustworthy, and user-controlled digital infrastructure.

Restack is deliberately broad: it covers technology from low-level hardware and middleware through local-first infrastructure and end-user applications.

### What they are looking for
Eligible work includes:
- Free and open-source software development
- Open standards and interoperability
- Software engineering and architecture
- Validation and testing
- Security and continuous integration
- Packaging and deployment
- Documentation and educational materials
- Usability and inclusive design
- Scientific research
- Governance and sustainability work connected to technical infrastructure

Projects are judged primarily on:
- Strategic relevance to the Open Internet Stack
- Technical quality
- Value for money

Applicants outside Europe can apply, but proposals from outside Horizon Europe countries need a clear European dimension.

### Potential FreeMoCap angle
Another case where we fit extremely will. We could easily adapt the PESOSE proposal to this. The only issue is that they state European applicants have priority, and to make their donors happy, non-European projects should still have a 'European dimension' (aka impact in Europe). I think we can make it work - because we have users in Europe, and we can specifically point to Jonathan Nillsson and Gibbet Games as an example of that impact.
